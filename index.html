Aim

To evaluate and dynamically resolve the total steady-state radial heat lost ($H$) through single-layer cylindrical pipe walls over a specific time interval ($t$) using variable geometric boundaries and structural thermal constants.

Core Mathematical Objectives

  • ▪ Compute steady-state radial heat transfer rates ($Q$) through cylindrical profiles.
  • ▪ Integrate temporal duration transformations ($t$) across standard unit baselines to find total heat loss accumulation ($H = Q \times t$).

Theory

Fourier's Law governing continuous steady-state heat conduction through radial geometries indicates that the total conductive surface area expands outward as a function of radius ($A = 2\pi rL$). Integrating this dependency across standard boundaries yields the heat flow rate ($Q$):

$$Q = \frac{2 \pi k L (T_1 - T_2)}{\ln\left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)}$$

To calculate the Total Heat Lost ($H$) over a designated operational time window ($t$), the steady-state continuous rate is scaled directly by the temporal quantity normalized to seconds:

$$H = Q \times t_{\text{seconds}}$$
Q = Heat flow rate calculation result (cal/sec)
t = Temporal tracking constraints (Sec/Min/Hrs)
k = Thermal conductivity coefficient (cal/cm·s·°C)
T₁ - T₂ = Direct operational temperature delta (°C)
